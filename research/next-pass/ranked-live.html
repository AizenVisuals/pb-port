    How battles work

    • It's real time. Your Pokémon fight on their own. You step in when you want to.
    • Call a move with the four move buttons. The fill shows its cooldown; a glow means it's ready and in range. A called move fires as soon as the current attack ends.
    • Switch by tapping a Pokémon on your bench. When one faints, you pick who comes in next.
    • Manual control: press Auto to switch to Manual and steer your Pokémon yourself or by dragging on the arena. It then only attacks when you press a move, and only dodges when you press Dodge: a quick dash the way you steer, once every 1.5 s. Press Manual again to hand it back.
    • Auto attacks: on Auto your Pokémon also picks its own moves. Don't want that? Turn off Auto attacks for me under your trainer (top right): it then still moves and dodges by itself, but only uses the moves you press.
    • Limited moves: a healing move restores 30% of max HP, and each Pokémon can heal twice in a battle. A stat-raising move (Swords Dance, Curse…) works twice each time a Pokémon is out: switch it out and back in to use it again. Protect waits 3 s longer after each use, back to 7 s on a switch.
    • Guard, on Auto and Manual: your Pokémon braces for half a second. Catch a hit in that window and it takes 65% less damage and little knockback, for as long as that attack keeps hitting (a whole Hyper Beam). It can't start once you're already being hit. On Manual a caught hit lets you guard again at once; a miss means a 3 s wait. Auto waits 3 s either way, and also guards by itself now and then, unless you turn off Auto guard for me under your trainer (top right).
    • Mega Evolve (or Greninja's Battle Bond) once per battle, while the Pokémon you chose is out.
    • Time limit: 4 minutes for 3v3, 7 for 6v6 (the clock at the top counts down). In the last quarter it turns into sudden death: every hit grows stronger, up to 4× at the end. If time still runs out, the team with more HP left wins.
    • Keys and controller: plug in a controller (Xbox, PlayStation, Switch Pro…) and press a button to battle with it. Change what every key and button does under .
    • Leaving a match: your team keeps fighting on its own, but if you're not back when it ends, you lose.

    How stats and moves work

    • Damage works as in the games: physical moves use Attack against Defense, special moves Sp. Atk against Sp. Def, with type matchups and a 1.5× bonus for a move of the user's own type. Critical hits (1 in 20) do 1.5×.
    • Speed doesn't shorten cooldowns. A faster Pokémon runs around the arena faster and is more likely to dodge an attack it sees coming (about Speed ÷ 260: roughly half the time at 130 Speed).
    • Cooldown: every move has its own wait before it can be used again, shown on its card and as the fill on its button. Stronger moves wait longer.
    • Reach: how far from the target a move can be used: close, mid range or long range (across the arena). A move button glows when the target is within its reach.
    • Accuracy is aim. Attacks are real shots, beams and lunges: they miss when the target moves out of the way or dodges. Moves marked can't be dodged always land.
    • Clashes: two beams fired at each other meet in the middle and push: the stronger move (higher power) slowly wins ground, and neither reaches its target while they clash. Two close-range lunges that meet head-on bounce both Pokémon back, and neither hits.
    • Evasion (Double Team) makes decoys: an attack can hit a decoy instead of the Pokémon.
    • Stat changes last until the Pokémon switches out: +1 is 1.5×, +2 is 2× and so on. Stat-raising moves work twice per stay on the field.
    • Weather (Rain Dance, Sunny Day, Sandstorm, Hail) lasts 18 seconds: rain powers up Water moves and weakens Fire, sun the other way round, sand and hail chip HP every 3 seconds.
    • Flinching is a brief stagger, and only when the target isn't in the middle of a move.
    • Status: a burn chips HP every 3 seconds and halves physical damage; poison chips more (Toxic more each time); paralysis makes the Pokémon move at about half speed, and 1 in 5 of its moves fails; sleep stops it for 2.5–5 seconds.
